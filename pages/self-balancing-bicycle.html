Autonomous Self-Balancing Bicycle

A self-balancing bicycle using a high-inertia reaction wheel and PID control.

Overview

From February to May 2026, I worked on a bicycle capable of dynamically balancing itself, using a high-inertia reaction wheel and PID control. The long-term goal is a self-balancing, driverless bicycle that can navigate autonomously indoors and outdoors; this phase focused on getting it to balance. Design & build

The heart of the system is an active reaction wheel, driven by a 24V 200W BLDC motor under STM32 control. I designed the lightweight steel wheel with a custom lightweighting pattern, had it cut on the waterjet, and finished it on the mill, along with a machined aluminum motor interface for high-inertia stabilization. Steering

I designed and built a custom bevel gear steering system so a servo can steer the bike. The bike's fork and head tube sit at an angle to the top tube, so off-the-shelf bevel gears wouldn't line up; I designed custom bevel gears at the matching angle and 3D printed them. The angled gears also let the servo sit as close as possible to the top tube, which keeps its weight near the frame for better stability. Next steps

The plan for the next phase is to turn the bike into a fully autonomous platform: convert the lightweight fixed-gear frame to electric drive with servo braking and a chain-driven motor, run a ROS2-based SLAM and planning stack on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano Super, use visual SLAM with RRT* for real-time mapping and motion planning in unknown environments, and add a 2D LiDAR for fast obstacle detection and avoidance.
